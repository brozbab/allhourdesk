Scenarios

Four calls, walked through.

These are illustrative scenarios, not customer case studies. AllHourDesk is early and onboarding its first properties — rather than dress that up with testimonials we haven't earned, here is exactly what the system does, step by step, in the situations it's built for.

Please read this first

Every scenario below is illustrative. The properties, callers and confirmation codes are composed for explanation — none of them describe a real guest or a real customer of ours, and none should be read as a performance claim. What is real is the mechanism: the sequence of steps each scenario describes is how the system actually behaves, and it has been run end to end against live hotel inventory and live payments.

01
Illustrative scenario · 03:12

The cancelled connection

A traveller is stranded when their late connection is cancelled. They search hotels near the airport and call the first three numbers. Two ring out. The third is a property running AllHourDesk with overnight forwarding enabled.

03:12:01   call answered · agent discloses it is an AI 03:12:24   dates captured · tonight → tomorrow · 1 guest 03:12:38   availability queried · 2 rates returned from PMS 03:13:02   guest selects flexible rate · room held 03:13:20   payment link sent by WhatsApp 03:16:04   paid · reservation confirmed · confirmation sent

What the morning shift sees: a confirmed booking already in the PMS, a transcript of the call, and a paid folio. No callback list, no held room to chase.

02
Illustrative scenario · 21:40

Two check-ins and a ringing phone

One receptionist is on duty. Two guests are checking in at once, and the phone rings for the fourth time in ten minutes. Historically that call either gets a rushed answer or none at all.

RING 1–4The desk line rings first, as always. The receptionist stays with the guests in front of them.
FALLTHROUGHUnanswered after the configured number of rings, the call forwards to AllHourDesk instead of ringing out.
HANDLEDThe caller wanted to know whether there's parking and whether they can arrive at midnight. Both answered from the property's own knowledge base.
LOGGEDThe exchange appears in the call log, so the desk knows what was said without having to ask.

The point: nothing was taken away from the front desk. The call that was going to be lost was the only one that changed hands.

03
Illustrative scenario · 02:05

A caller who doesn't speak the local language

A Spanish-speaking guest calls a property in Ireland. At 02:00 there is no one on site who speaks Spanish, and the caller's English is limited to a few words.

Detected, not selected

The language is picked up from how the caller speaks. There's no "press 2 for Spanish" menu to navigate in a language they don't read.

Disclosed in their language

The AI disclosure is given in Spanish, not in the property's default language.

Same five operations

Availability, hold, payment link and confirmation work identically — the language changes, the mechanism doesn't.

Written record

The transcript is available to staff afterwards regardless of the language it was conducted in.

04
Illustrative scenario · 23:50

The booking that has to be cancelled

A guest booked two nights and now can't travel. They call late, expecting to leave a message and hope somebody acts on it before the cancellation window closes.

01

Identify

The agent looks up the reservation and confirms the caller's identity against the booking before doing anything to it.

02

State the terms

The cancellation policy actually attached to that rate is read back — not a general policy, the one on their booking.

03

Cancel and refund

The reservation is cancelled in the PMS and the refund is triggered through the same payment provider that took it.

04

Inventory returns

The room goes back on sale immediately rather than at the start of the next shift.

Why it matters commercially: a room released at 23:50 can still be sold for that night. The same room released at 09:00 usually can't.
