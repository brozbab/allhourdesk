# AllHourDesk Website Content & Information Architecture

_Last updated: 21 September 2026_

## 1. Purpose of this document

This document defines the recommended website structure, positioning, messaging, and draft content for the next version of **AllHourDesk**.

The goal is to move the website from an early-product/technical-demo feel to a **credible, professional B2B SaaS company presence** that gives hotel owners, general managers, operations leaders, front-office leaders, and technology buyers confidence that AllHourDesk is a serious vendor they can evaluate and trust.

The site should make three things immediately clear:

1. **What AllHourDesk is**
2. **What business problem it solves**
3. **Why a hotel can trust it with guest calls, reservations, payments, and operational workflows**

---

# 2. Current site assessment

## What already works well

The current website has several strong foundations:

- The problem is easy to understand: hotels miss calls overnight and during busy periods.
- The distinction between a simple answering service and a transaction-capable platform is strong.
- The current copy explains the booking flow clearly: availability -> hold -> payment -> confirmation.
- The product is honest about being early-stage and avoids fake customer logos or testimonials.
- The focus on safe failure handling is unusually good and technically credible.
- The existing site already explains multilingual calling, PMS integration, payments, call forwarding, and compliance considerations.
- The current scenario-based storytelling helps prospects imagine real operational use.

## What should change

The current site reads more like a sophisticated founder/product narrative than a mature vendor website.

The biggest issues are:

### 2.1 The positioning is too narrow around the "night shift"

"Your night manager never sleeps" is memorable, but it risks positioning AllHourDesk as a niche overnight answering tool.

The product is broader:

- 24/7 guest call handling
- overflow support
- direct reservations
- existing reservation management
- multilingual service
- payments
- FAQs and property information
- PMS-connected transactions
- operational handover and audit trails

The new website should retain the night-call story as an important use case, but position AllHourDesk as an **AI-powered hotel guest communications and reservation operations platform**.

### 2.2 The homepage does not establish vendor trust quickly enough

A serious hotel buyer wants immediate answers to questions such as:

- Is this a real company?
- Where is it based?
- Who is behind it?
- What systems does it integrate with?
- How is data handled?
- How are payments handled?
- What happens if the AI fails?
- Can I start safely without changing my existing phone number?
- Does it replace staff?
- What countries are supported?
- How does implementation work?
- Is support available?
- What is the commercial model?

The current site answers some of these, but they are spread across pages and written as product explanations rather than buyer reassurance.

### 2.3 The language sometimes feels defensive

Examples of the current tone include phrases such as:

- "Come and break it."
- "The fair objections."
- "We'd rather you hear this from us..."
- "No projected revenue figures..."
- "The awkward questions..."

These are refreshing in a founder conversation, but they make the company sound experimental.

The revised tone should remain transparent while feeling:

- established
- calm
- operationally competent
- enterprise-aware
- hospitality-specific
- security-conscious

### 2.4 The information architecture is product-led, not buyer-led

The current navigation:

- Product
- Benefits
- Scenarios
- About
- Contact

is understandable, but a corporate B2B buyer generally expects:

- Platform
- Solutions
- Integrations
- Security / Trust
- Company
- Resources
- Book a demo

This allows prospects to investigate the product according to their own buying questions.

### 2.5 Missing proof and trust architecture

Until customer case studies exist, the site should compensate with verifiable proof:

- Supported / live integrations
- Product workflow diagrams
- Screenshots
- architecture / integration explanations
- legal company details
- implementation model
- data/privacy documentation
- security practices
- named founders
- clear support channels
- pilot process
- real product demo

Do not invent certifications, customers, metrics, SLA numbers, uptime claims, or compliance certifications that do not yet exist.

# 4. Core messaging pillars

## 4.1 Never miss a booking-ready guest

Calls that arrive overnight, during check-in peaks, or while staff are busy can be answered immediately instead of ringing out.

## 4.2 Complete the task, not just the conversation

AllHourDesk is designed to take operational actions through connected hotel systems — such as checking availability, creating a reservation, cancelling a booking, or sending a payment link.

## 4.3 Work with the hotel's existing operation

Start with call forwarding and existing phone numbers. Connect to the hotel's PMS and supporting systems instead of forcing staff to manage a new source of truth.

## 4.4 Multilingual by default

Guests speak naturally. AllHourDesk can detect and continue in the caller's language without IVR-style language menus.

## 4.5 Designed around safe transactions

Inventory, bookings, payment status, retries, failure handling, and audit history should be explained as controlled workflows rather than AI improvisation.

## 4.6 Human team remains in control

Position the product as coverage, overflow, automation, and operational support — not as "replace your receptionist."

# 21. FAQ page

URL: **/faq**

Recommended questions:

## Is AllHourDesk an answering service?

No. AllHourDesk is software designed to handle guest conversations and, where configured, complete operational workflows through connected hotel systems.

## Does it replace my front desk?

The recommended starting model is overflow and after-hours coverage. The hotel's own team can continue to answer first.

## Can we keep our existing phone number?

Yes. Initial deployments can use call forwarding or existing telephony routing. Other deployment models can be considered later.

## Which PMS platforms do you support?

Apaleo is currently integrated. Support for additional systems is being expanded. Contact AllHourDesk to confirm your PMS.

## Can AllHourDesk take bookings?

Where the required PMS and payment integrations are configured, AllHourDesk can support a booking workflow using live availability and valid rates.

## How does payment work?

A secure hosted payment link can be sent to the guest by WhatsApp or SMS so the guest does not need to speak card details during the call.

## What happens if the PMS or another integration is unavailable?

AllHourDesk is designed to avoid inventing operational information. The interaction follows a configured fallback or escalation path when required data or actions are unavailable.

## Does it support multiple languages?

AllHourDesk supports multilingual conversations. Production language coverage should be confirmed during onboarding.

## Can calls be recorded?

Recording behaviour and retention depend on configuration, deployment and applicable requirements. These settings are agreed during onboarding.

## How long does implementation take?

Do not publish a fixed timeframe until the onboarding process is repeatable enough to support an SLA-like statement.

Recommended wording:

> The implementation plan depends on PMS access, telephony setup and the workflows being enabled. We will confirm the onboarding plan after a technical discovery session.

## Can we pilot it first?

Yes. A limited pilot using forwarding on no-answer or selected operating hours is the recommended starting point.

The site should visually communicate:

- professional SaaS company
- hospitality expertise
- reliable infrastructure
- premium but approachable
- European corporate credibility
- modern AI technology without looking experimental

## 24.2 Avoid the generic "AI startup" aesthetic

Avoid:

- excessive glowing gradients
- robot imagery
- humanoid AI illustrations
- floating brain/network graphics
- overuse of dark hacker-style interfaces
- fake dashboard screenshots
- stock imagery of call-centre headsets

## 24.3 Use product visuals as proof

Prioritise:

- call workflow diagram
- reservation flow
- integration architecture
- sample call timeline
- booking confirmation flow
- hotel knowledge configuration
- operational handover screen
- real admin UI screenshots as the product matures

## 24.4 Hospitality imagery

Use high-quality photography sparingly:

- hotel reception
- evening arrival
- boutique/independent hotel
- business traveller
- guest using mobile phone
- real hospitality environments

Images should support the narrative rather than dominate the page.

# 26. Claims discipline

All website claims should be categorised internally as one of:

**LIVE** — working in production or production-ready today  
**PILOT** — available to controlled design partners  
**PLANNED** — roadmap  
**VISION** — strategic direction

Do not mix these categories in marketing copy.

Particularly avoid unsupported claims around:

- uptime
- response latency
- booking conversion uplift
- staffing savings
- revenue uplift
- number of languages
- number of integrations
- GDPR "compliance" as a blanket statement
- PCI certification
- EU AI Act compliance certification
- customer count
- geographic data residency
- enterprise readiness
- 24/7 human support

Trust comes from precision, not from sounding bigger than the company currently is.

# 30. Final recommendation

The strongest characteristic of the current AllHourDesk site is that it explains the mechanics honestly and clearly. Do not lose that.

The redesign should **keep the technical credibility while changing the framing**:

### Current impression

> "A smart early-stage AI product that has solved an interesting hotel night-call problem."

### Desired impression

> "A focused hospitality technology company with a credible platform, clear operational controls, professional implementation model, trustworthy architecture and a product worth piloting."

The company does not need to pretend to be large.

It needs to look **deliberate, precise, stable and trustworthy**.

That is the strongest corporate position for AllHourDesk at its current stage.
